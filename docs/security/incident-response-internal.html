Incident Response Policy

Effective date: January 1, 2026
Owner: Chief Technology Officer
Applies to: sayhii production systems, cloud infrastructure, customer data, internal systems, and third-party services

1. Purpose​

sayhii is committed to protecting the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of its systems and data. This Incident Response Policy defines the process for identifying, responding to, mitigating, and documenting security incidents that may impact the sayhii platform, customer data, or internal systems.

This policy supports sayhii’s Data Privacy and Retention practices by ensuring that security incidents involving personal, survey, or operational data are handled in a consistent and controlled manner.

2. Scope​

This policy applies to:

  • All sayhii production environments
  • Cloud infrastructure, applications, and data stores
  • Customer data, including personally identifiable information (PII), survey responses, and anonymized or aggregated data
  • Internal systems, credentials, and endpoints
  • Third-party services used to operate or deliver the sayhii platform

3. Definition of a Security Incident​

A security incident is any actual or suspected event that compromises, or could reasonably compromise, the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of sayhii systems or data.

Examples include, but are not limited to:

  • Unauthorized access to systems or data
  • Exposure or suspected exposure of customer data
  • Compromised credentials, API keys, or secrets
  • Malware or suspicious activity on company devices
  • Misconfigurations resulting in unintended public access
  • Denial-of-service or malicious service disruption

When uncertainty exists, the event should be treated as a security incident and escalated for review.

4. Roles and Responsibilities​

Due to the size of the organization, individuals may fulfill multiple roles during an incident.

RoleResponsibility
Incident LeadOverall ownership of incident response, decision-making, and coordination
Technical ResponderInvestigation, containment, remediation, and recovery actions
Communications OwnerInternal and external communications, including customer notifications
Executive Decision MakerApproval of major decisions, notifications, and disclosures

The Incident Lead is responsible for ensuring this policy is followed and that incidents are appropriately documented.

5. Incident Severity Classification​

Incidents are classified to guide prioritization and response. Severity may be adjusted as more information becomes available.

SeverityDescription
LowNo data exposure and minimal operational impact
MediumLimited system or data impact with no confirmed external exposure
HighConfirmed data exposure or system compromise
CriticalSignificant data exposure, regulatory impact, or widespread service disruption

6. Incident Response Process​

6.1 Identification​

Security incidents may be identified through monitoring, automated alerts, customer reports, vendor notifications, or internal discovery. Upon identification, the following must be recorded:

  • Date and time of discovery
  • Individual reporting or discovering the incident
  • Affected systems and data (if known)
  • Initial severity assessment

6.2 Containment​

Immediate actions are taken to limit the scope and impact of the incident. These may include:

  • Revoking or rotating credentials and secrets
  • Disabling affected accounts or services
  • Restricting network access
  • Preserving logs and evidence

Containment actions take priority over root-cause analysis.

6.3 Investigation​

An investigation is conducted to determine:

  • What occurred
  • When the incident began
  • How the incident occurred
  • What systems or data were affected

Relevant logs, audit trails, and system records are reviewed and preserved in accordance with the Data Privacy and Retention Policy.

6.4 Eradication and Remediation​

Following investigation:

  • The root cause is addressed
  • Vulnerabilities are patched or configurations corrected
  • Preventive controls are implemented to reduce likelihood of recurrence

6.5 Recovery​

Systems are restored to normal operation, if impacted, and verified to be functioning securely before resuming standard operations.

6.6 Lessons Learned​

After resolution, a post-incident review is conducted to document:

  • Incident timeline
  • Root cause
  • Impact assessment
  • Remediation actions
  • Preventive improvements

Findings are used to improve security controls, policies, and procedures.

7. Communication and Notification​

7.1 Internal Communication​

Incident communications are centralized and limited to necessary stakeholders. Communications should be factual, time-stamped, and avoid speculation.

7.2 Customer Communication​

Customers are notified when:

  • Their data is confirmed to be exposed, or
  • Service availability is materially impacted

Notifications include, as appropriate:

  • A high-level description of the incident
  • The type of data impacted, if any
  • Actions taken by sayhii
  • Recommended customer actions, if applicable

Communications are coordinated to ensure accuracy and consistency.

8. Breach Notification​

If a security incident is determined to constitute a data breach involving personal data, sayhii will:

  • Assess the scope and nature of the breach
  • Determine notification obligations based on contractual, legal, and regulatory requirements
  • Notify affected customers without undue delay, consistent with applicable laws and agreements
  • Coordinate notifications to regulators or authorities when required

Breach notifications are aligned with sayhii’s Data Privacy and Retention Policy and are limited to verified facts available at the time of notification.

9. Third-Party Incidents​

If a security incident originates from a third-party service provider:

  • The impact to sayhii systems and customer data is assessed
  • Vendor communications are monitored and documented
  • Customers are notified if their data or access is affected
  • All response actions are recorded

10. Documentation and Evidence Retention​

For each security incident, sayhii maintains documentation including:

  • Incident summary
  • Timeline of response actions
  • Relevant logs or evidence
  • Root cause analysis
  • Preventive measures implemented

Incident records are retained in accordance with the Data Privacy and Retention Policy, with a minimum retention period of 12 months.

11. Testing and Review​

This policy is reviewed at least annually and after any significant security incident. Incident response procedures may be tested through tabletop exercises or simulated scenarios to ensure effectiveness.

12. Policy Approval​

This Incident Response Policy is approved by management and applies to all personnel and systems within scope.
